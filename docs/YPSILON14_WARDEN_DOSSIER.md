# THE HAUNTING OF YPSILON 14 — WARDEN'S DOSSIER

*A play aid for D. G. Chapman's one-shot (Tuesday Knight Games), configured as
the tutorial module for a campaign continuing on Samsa IV. All text here is
original; buy the original module.*

---

## THE SHAPE OF THE SESSION

Three to four hours. The players arrive on a job with a deadline, discover the
job is not the point, and have to decide what they are willing to spend time on.

| Beat | Roughly | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Docking, Sonya, the pallets | 0:00–0:30 | Establish the job and the clock |
| Wandering the base, talking | 0:30–1:30 | Meet ten people, collect wrongness |
| First tell — the cat, the dust, a tape | 1:00–1:45 | The base stops being a workplace |
| Somebody is taken | 1:30–2:15 | The crew become frightened and gather |
| Down the mine | 2:00–3:00 | The pod, the goo, SILENCE, the empty suit |
| The decision | 3:00+ | Kill it, deny it, blow it, or run |

**The job is six pallets at twenty minutes each.** Two hours of the session, if
they do it. This is the module's central squeeze and you should never resolve
it for them: the cargo is medicine for the next module's colony, and the time
it costs is time somebody on this base does not survive.

---

## THE TRUTH, IN ORDER

1. Nine weeks ago the drills opened an unsurveyed cavity: smooth black rock in
   arches, and a pod with something asleep in it. Sonya logged yellow residue.
2. Five weeks ago the Company sent Dr Ethan Giovanni — a xenobiologist with a
   geologist's job title — to open it.
3. Three and a half weeks ago he cut the pod. It woke. He did not report this.
4. Nine days ago Kantaro took goo on bare skin in the depths and told nobody.
5. Six days ago Giovanni infected himself deliberately and recorded the log
   while it happened. He has been dead for about thirty hours.
6. Two nights ago Mike Voss — already infected — tore the shower out of the
   washroom wall because he could not bear to be near water.
7. Last night Mike took a laser cutter into the depths, burned **SILENCE** into
   the rock as a warning to whoever came next, and was eaten inside his suit.
8. This morning the players dock for a four-hour cargo transfer.

**Mike wrote SILENCE. The creature writes nothing.** It does not communicate,
plan revenge, or taunt. Never let it become a character.

---

## THE THING, AND WHAT IT WANTS

Four drives, checked in this order. The engine runs them; you narrate them.

**MEND.** Wounded, it goes to the pod and nothing else matters. The goo repairs
its Health, never its Hits. If the pod is destroyed it cannot mend — and it
stops being careful, which is worse for everyone.

**FEED.** Hunger climbs steadily. It wants somebody alone. Two people it will
consider. Three or more it watches and leaves. When the base musters, the only
isolated warm things left are the players.

**DRY.** It will not cross standing water. Running the showers denies it a
compartment for two hours. A thrown bottle drives it off entirely.

**QUIET.** Blind, hunting by echolocation. Noise draws it and confuses it —
both at once. A recorded decoy left playing in an empty room buys ninety
minutes of it being somewhere else.

### Telling them without showing them
Dust swept into a long wide track. A vaccsuit hook swinging on an empty wall. A
smell of hot copper and standing water. A radio call unanswered. Their own
breath coming back off a surface that should not be that close. **And the cat.**

### The five ways it ends
1. **Kill it.** Three hits or forty damage — but it defends with Advantage
   while unseen, breaks off the moment it is hurt, and mends at the pod. The
   only reliable route is: infrared, then deny it the pod, then corner it.
2. **Deny it.** Reverse the slurry pump from the workspace terminal and flood
   the lower workings. Forty minutes, one keycard, and you are never in the
   room. This is the intended clever solution — reward it enormously.
3. **Blow the base.** Sonya's keycard, thirty minutes, everything gone
   including the cargo and anyone still inside.
4. **Seal and report.** Lock the airlocks, tell the Company, leave it in the
   quiet it always wanted. Somebody will open it later. Not them.
5. **Run.** It comes with you. It has been listening to your docking clamps all
   day. This is an earned outcome, not a punishment — play it straight.

## THE CREW

Everyone has a public answer, a private answer, and a secret. The engine gives
them posts, haunts, and someone they care about; they move on their own, and
when they are frightened they stop being alone.

| | Public | Secret |
|---|---|---|
| **SONYA** Team Leader | Runs the base, wants you gone on schedule | Has already written the letter that closes the site. Believes Mike walked out of an airlock, because the alternative is unbearable |
| **ROSA** Engineer | Blunt, doing two jobs, furious | Keeping dated notes for a tribunal. Will believe anything you can prove and nothing you can't |
| **DANA** Head Driller | Stoic, facts only | Seeing Kantaro. Heard something below the tunnel three weeks ago and ran |
| **KANTARO** Loader | Hasn't washed in days | Infected nine days ago. Feels tremendous. Has hours |
| **JEROME** Asst Driller | Jokes, badly, faster each week | Something moves in his ceiling. Unlicensed handgun under the pillow |
| **ASHRAF** Breaker | Eager, agrees with everyone | Nothing. He is exactly what he appears to be, which is what makes him dangerous to send anywhere |
| **MORGAN** Loader | Warm, nervous, cat guy | Has watched Prince refuse the workspace for two weeks and never said it out loud |
| **RIE** Putter | Sarcastic, then useful | Saw Giovanni come up carrying nothing. Was somewhere she shouldn't have been |
| **PRINCE** the cat | Hates baths | Can see it perfectly well and has been managing around it for three weeks |

**Pet Prince twice and he follows you.** He will not enter a room the creature
is in. A table that works this out has built a monster detector from a cat, and
you should let it work every single time.

### The moments to play for
- **Kantaro comes apart** in front of whoever is standing nearest. Do not
  foreshadow it more than the module already has.
- **Someone is taken while they are talking to a player.** Mid-sentence, a
  sound like a bath emptying, a wet drag towards the ducting, and no mark on
  the deck.
- **Ashraf volunteers.** Let the players hear how cheerfully he agrees. Let
  them decide. Do not warn them, and do not punish them for it either.
- **Dana goes into the mine alone** after Kantaro dies, and does not come back.
- **Jerome fires at a noise** in a corridor with people in it — unless somebody
  took that handgun from his bunk hours earlier and never knew why it mattered.

## THE COMPANY

Traffic control is forty minutes each way and will send a receipt and no
instructions — unless the players mention biological material, in which case
the reply arrives in twenty-two minutes and says: *preserve all biological
material, do not destroy the site, await instruction.*

Giovanni's sealed case holds his orders: **recover viable biological material.
Priority over site operations. Priority over personnel. Priority over any
vessel in dock.** That document is the single most useful object on the rock,
and it is the crew's only leverage in the modules that follow.

## RUNNING IT AS A TUTORIAL

Five habits, in the order the module teaches them:

1. **The clock costs more than the dice.** Say the time out loud. "That's
   twenty minutes." Every time.
2. **Ask for a roll only when failure costs something,** and let the cost be
   time. New players learn faster from a base that keeps moving than from a
   failed check that stops the scene.
3. **Stress compounds and Panic is the killer.** Point at the Stress track the
   first time it moves. Let them see a Panic Check before anything is chasing
   them.
4. **The monster is a problem, not a hit-point pool.** The first encounter
   should be escapable and should teach one property — water, or noise, or that
   shooting an invisible thing goes badly.
5. **The Company is not your ally.** Play the twenty-two-minute receipt
   absolutely straight and let them draw the conclusion themselves.

If a character dies, there are more of the crew asleep on the ship. Thaw one.
Death should cost the party a person they liked, not the player their evening.

## HANDOFF TO *ANOTHER BUG HUNT*

The debrief screen records what carries forward:

- **Pallets loaded (0–6).** Greta Base's only medical stock inside four weeks.
- **Ypsilon survivors taken off.** Ten more mouths, and ten more witnesses.
- **Anyone infected.** They have hours, and the ship is nine days out.
- **The specimen case.** Whether the Company's orders left the rock, and who
  is holding them.
- **What the Company was told.** They will be asked about this on Samsa IV, and
  the answer they gave here is the one that will be read back to them.
